How to Reset Network Settings on an iPhone

If your iPhone struggles to join Wi-Fi, loses its cellular connection, or no longer connects to a Bluetooth device, resetting its network settings may help. This process restores the phone’s built-in network preferences without deleting your apps, photos, messages, or other personal content.

When to Reset Network Settings

  • Your iPhone cannot find or connect to a Wi-Fi network.
  • Wi-Fi appears connected, but webpages and apps do not load.
  • Cellular data, mobile hotspots, or Bluetooth connections are unreliable.
  • A VPN, DNS configuration, or other network setting is causing connection problems.

Start with simpler solutions, such as restarting the iPhone, toggling Wi-Fi or Airplane Mode, or checking whether your internet service is working. A network reset is most useful when basic troubleshooting has not resolved the issue.

What the Reset Removes

  • Saved Wi-Fi networks and their passwords.
  • Stored Wi-Fi preferences and network-related settings.
  • Cellular network preferences.
  • DNS configurations.
  • VPN settings and configurations.

You will need to reconnect to Wi-Fi and manually re-enter network passwords. VPNs and custom DNS configurations may also need to be set up again. The reset normally does not remove installed apps or erase the iPhone’s general data.

Prepare Before Resetting

  • Make sure you know the iPhone passcode, because you will be asked to enter it.
  • Record any important Wi-Fi passwords, especially for a home or work network.
  • Note the details of VPN, DNS, hotspot, or corporate network configurations.
  • Check that your iPhone is charged and connected to a reliable power source.

How to Reset Network Settings

  • Open the Settings app on your iPhone.
  • Select General.
  • Scroll to the bottom and choose Transfer or Reset iPhone. On older versions of iOS, look for Reset in the General menu instead.
  • Tap Reset, then select Reset Network Settings.
  • Review the warning and confirm that you want to continue.
  • Enter your iPhone passcode if prompted. Your iPhone will close and restart automatically.

The reset usually takes only a few minutes. If your device is managed through a work or school system, network settings may be restored by its administrator rather than by you.

What to Do After the Reset

  • Wait for the iPhone to finish restarting and return to the Home Screen.
  • Open Settings and select Wi-Fi.
  • Choose your network from the list and enter its password if necessary.
  • Test a webpage in Safari and check that any app requiring internet access works.
  • Restore your VPN, custom DNS, Bluetooth, hotspot, or other network settings as needed.

If the Problem Continues

  • Restart the iPhone again after reconnecting to Wi-Fi.
  • Check that Airplane Mode is off and Wi-Fi or Cellular Data is enabled.
  • Try another Wi-Fi network or ask another device to connect to the same network.
  • Confirm that your mobile plan is active and that there is no local carrier outage.
  • Check for an available iOS update in Settings under General and Software Update.
  • Contact your network provider if several devices have trouble connecting to the same service.

If the issue affects only one app, update or reinstall that app before resetting the entire network configuration. If problems remain, Apple Support or your network provider can help determine whether the fault lies with the iPhone, the account, the app, or the connection.

Network Reset Versus Erase All Content and Settings

A network reset is a targeted troubleshooting step. Erase All Content and Settings is much more comprehensive: it removes the device’s data and settings, including the Apple Account sign-in, and returns the iPhone to its initial setup process. Use the full erase only when you intend to set the phone up again from scratch or have been advised to do so by Apple Support.
