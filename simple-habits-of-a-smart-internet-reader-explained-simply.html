Simple habits of a smart internet reader, explained simply

The web rewards readers who slow down, ask questions, and know how to weigh what they discover.

  • Authority: Is the author or organization reputable? Do they have expertise in the subject matter?
  • Bias: Is the source known for a particular bias or agenda? Is the information presented in a balanced way?
  • Accuracy: Are the claims supported by evidence or other credible sources?
  • Step Away: Take short breaks to clear your mind and return to the content with fresh eyes.
  • Summarize: Write a brief summary of the main points to help consolidate the information.
  • Reflect: Consider how the information affects your understanding of the topic and whether it changes your perspective.

Clickbait headlines are designed to grab your attention and entice you to click on a link. While they can be harmless, they frequently lead to low-quality content or misinformation. To avoid falling into the clickbait trap, consider the following:

  • Evaluate Headlines: If a headline seems too sensational or outrageous, it probably is.
  • Read Beyond the Headline: Sometimes, the content of the article doesn't match the promise of the headline.
  • Check the URL: Be wary of URLs that mimic reputable websites but have slight variations.
