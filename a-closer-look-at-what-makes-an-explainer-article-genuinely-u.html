An explainer article should be engaging to capture and hold the reader's attention. This can be achieved through a variety of means, such as using anecdotes, asking thought-provoking questions, or incorporating relevant examples. The tone of the article should be approachable and relatable, making the reader feel as though they are having a conversation with the author rather than being lectured at.

One of the most important aspects of a useful explainer article is its clarity. The primary goal of such an article is to break down complex information into digestible pieces. This means using simple language and avoiding jargon whenever possible. If technical terms are unavoidable, they should be clearly defined within the context of the article. The structure of the article should also be straightforward, with a logical flow that guides the reader from one point to the next without confusion.

For example, if the article is explaining a scientific concept, it should begin with a basic introduction to the topic before diving into more detailed explanations. This approach helps readers build a solid foundation of understanding before moving on to more complex ideas.

A genuinely useful explainer article should cover the topic thoroughly. This doesn't mean it has to be exhaustive, but it should provide a comprehensive overview that answers the most common questions a reader might have. The article should address the key aspects of the topic, including its background, main points, and any relevant examples or case studies.

Additionally, the article should be accessible to a wide audience. This means considering the diverse backgrounds and levels of knowledge of potential readers. The article should be written in a way that is understandable to someone who is new to the topic, while still providing value to those who are more familiar with it.

Finally, a useful explainer article should provide actionable takeaways. After reading the article, readers should have a clear idea of what steps they can take next, whether it's further research, trying out a new technique, or simply having a better understanding of the topic. This not only adds value to the article but as well empowers readers to apply what they've learned in a practical way.

Similarly, a chart can be useful for comparing different data points or showing trends over time.
