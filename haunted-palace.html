Haunted Palace: A Modern Korean Fantasy Explained

Haunted Palace is a South Korean fantasy television series that combines ghost stories, reincarnation, romance, comedy, and an adventure set against the ruins of an ancient kingdom. Adapted from Chon Kang’s webtoon, the drama is more than a conventional haunted-house mystery. Its ghosts are connected to memory, identity, power, and unfinished duties from the past. This overview is spoiler-light, but it provides context for the series’ central mythology and characters.

The Story Without Major Spoilers

High-school student Yoon Yeo-kyung can see spirits, an ability that makes her ordinary life frightening and isolating. When she encounters Yeon Ho-gyeong, a monk tied to the fallen kingdom of Yeoju, she is drawn into a conflict that long predates her own generation. The story shifts between the modern world and a historical-inspired realm populated by demons, ghosts, and people whose identities have been carried across time.

The key premise is not simply that a palace is haunted. It is that the past remains active: characters inherit old bodies, grudges, loyalties, and responsibilities. Yeo-kyung becomes a bridge between eras, while the royal figures must decide whether survival means repeating history or challenging it. Although the drama includes genuinely eerie scenes, its tone also moves toward swashbuckling adventure and romantic fantasy.

Main Characters and Their Roles

  • Yoon Yeo-kyung, played by Yuna Ha, is the modern protagonist whose ability to see ghosts makes her the story’s link between living people and spirits.
  • Yeon Ho-gyeong, played by Go Soo, is the central historical figure and ally to Yeo-kyung. His connection to Yeoju gives the conflict its political and emotional weight.
  • Baek Gwi-ryeol, played by Yook Sung-jae, brings a different kind of power and perspective to the group, complicating the heroes’ assumptions about the ancient world.
